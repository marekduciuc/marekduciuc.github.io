The situation

Elasticsearch is easy to start and hard to run well. The same problems kept coming back: indexing that could not keep up, reindexing that took days, clusters with too many shards, nodes dropping out under load, data that did not match its mapping – and logs that needed one secure, searchable place that the people responsible could actually use.

I worked on these problems from three sides: as a support engineer at Elastic, as a consultant tuning customer clusters, and as an engineer building and optimising platforms on contract.

Speed: measure, then tune

Before changing anything I wrote an automated performance test with the Rally benchmarking framework. It set the baseline and ran again after every optimisation stage, so each change was judged by numbers, not by feel.

  • Indexing: moving to bulk indexing raised throughput from 20 to nearly 1,800 operations per second.
  • Reindexing: a full reindex went from 3 days to a few hours.
  • Search: querying boolean flags instead of comparing integer values, and querying fewer fields, cut search latency by 20%.

Stability and running costs

As a consultant I improved the performance and stability of customer clusters:

  • Mapping and query optimisation, and sharding changes.
  • Deployment sizing recommendations.
  • Hot-warm architecture, so recent data sits on fast nodes and older data on cheaper ones.
  • Automated data purge with index lifecycle management (ILM) and snapshots with snapshot lifecycle management (SLM).

Customers achieved significant reductions in operational costs as a result.

Central log management for a bank

For a Czech bank (name under NDA) I built a central log management solution handling gigabytes of logs a day.

  • Ingest: pipelines with Beats and Logstash.
  • Access: role-based access with role mapping to Active Directory groups, so each team sees what it should and nothing else.
  • Visibility: dashboards and visualisations in Kibana.
  • Operations: Elasticsearch management and administration.

Teaching the people who use it

I trained the bank's internal and external users in Kibana: one-to-one video sessions with team leads, walking through the dashboards I had prepared and how to use them in their daily work.

Production support under pressure

For three years I was a Support Engineer at Elastic, helping customers and prospects with every part of the stack – Elasticsearch, Kibana, Logstash and Beats – including production outages handled live with the customer on a video call. Typical cases:

  • Authentication and authorisation problems.
  • Ingestion failures, mostly data that did not match its mapping.
  • Oversharding.
  • Nodes disconnecting because they were overloaded.

I also proposed improvements to Elastic products; several of them were implemented. Since then I have kept working with the technology: an Elasticsearch product search backend in Java at Conrad Electronic, and OpenSearch as the event store of an e-commerce fulfilment centre.

Results

  • Indexing throughput from 20 to nearly 1,800 operations per second.
  • Full reindex from 3 days to a few hours; search latency down by 20%.
  • Significant operational cost reductions for consulting customers.
  • A bank's logs – gigabytes a day – in one place, with access tied to Active Directory groups.
  • Production outages handled live with customers, and product improvements adopted by Elastic.

What I would do differently

The Kibana training covered using the dashboards I had prepared, not building new ones. Team leads could read their data, but every new view came back to me. Next time I would teach them to build their own visualisations and metrics, so the platform keeps growing after the handover.

What this means for your platform

  • Measure before you tune: a benchmark baseline turns opinions into numbers.
  • Fix the data model, the queries and the shard layout before adding hardware.
  • Automate the data lifecycle, so storage costs do not creep up.
  • Tie access to your directory groups from day one.
  • Hand over to people who can build on the platform, not only read it.
