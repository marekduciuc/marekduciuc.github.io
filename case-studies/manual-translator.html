From a paper manual to a Czech document: AI only where it is needed

A tool I built for an e-commerce shop: photos of a paper manual in English or Polish go in, an editable Czech document comes out. Every step that does not need AI runs locally; the AI only reads and translates the page.

The situation

The shop has to give its customers Czech manuals. Suppliers often do not send an electronic version at all – only the original paper manual, in English or Polish. Until now, someone retyped and translated these manuals by hand.

What the tool does

  • Input: photos or scans of the paper manual – PDF, JPG, PNG or TIFF.
  • Output: an editable Czech document, exported to DOCX or PDF, with headings, lists and tables.
  • Editing: the translation opens in an editor inside the tool. Your own photos and pictures can be added there, or later in Word.

The original illustrations are not copied over; the user adds the pictures they want to keep.

Messy input is fine

The pages do not have to be scanned neatly:

  • Sideways or upside down: the orientation is detected and corrected.
  • Slightly crooked: a photo taken at a small angle is straightened.
  • Two pages in one photo: booklet spreads are detected and split.
  • Out of order: pages are sorted by their page numbers; anything left can be moved up or down by hand.
  • Noise and margins: noisy scans are cleaned and empty margins cropped.

AI only where it is needed

Every call to an AI model costs time and money and adds a step that can go wrong. So each step asks one question: does this need AI? Most do not.

  • Local: noise removal, orientation, straightening, splitting spreads, sorting, cropping, language detection and OCR.
  • AI: for each page, Claude gets the cleaned page image and the text from the local OCR. It corrects OCR mistakes against the image and translates the page into Czech.
  • Language first: the language of each page is detected locally. Pages in languages the user did not select are never sent to the AI.
  • OCR text as a head start: giving the model the OCR text, instead of asking it to write everything from the image, cuts the output tokens by an order of magnitude.
  • The right weight of tool: local OCR takes a few seconds per page on an ordinary CPU. A heavier GPU-based OCR service would have taken minutes per page for the same job.

Guardrails

  • Retries with back-off when the AI service is busy or fails.
  • A cache of translated pages, so nothing is translated twice.
  • A cap on the output size of each call.
  • File type checks and upload limits per session.
  • An automated test suite for the pipeline and the API.

How it evolved

The first version was a script that sent everything to the AI. It worked, but it was slow and paid for steps that ordinary code does better. Step by step the preprocessing moved into local code, and a user interface came on top, so the shop's staff can upload, check and edit the result themselves.

How the work changed

  • Before: the call-centre team retyped each paper manual and translated it by hand.
  • Now: the same team photographs the manual, uploads it, checks and edits the Czech result in the tool and adds the pictures they need.
  • Effect: by the team's own account, a manual now takes an order of magnitude less of their time. It has not been measured more precisely.

Results

  • Paper manuals in English or Polish become editable Czech documents without retyping – the call-centre team spends an order of magnitude less time on them.
  • Phone photos are enough: rotated, slightly crooked or shuffled pages are handled.
  • The AI does one job per page – read and translate – and nothing else.
  • The result can be finished in the tool or in Word, with the shop's own pictures.

What I would do differently

I would start where I ended: AI only for the translation, everything else local from day one. Moving steps out of the AI later meant rebuilding parts of the pipeline that a clear split at the start would have avoided.

What this means for your team

  • Ask of every step whether it needs AI. Most preprocessing does not.
  • Give the model a head start – such as OCR text – instead of making it do everything.
  • Filter before you send: work that should not be translated should never reach the model.
  • Let people finish the result in tools they already use.
