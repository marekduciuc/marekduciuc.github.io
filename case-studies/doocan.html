The situation

I ran the day-to-day operations of an e-shop's fulfilment centre: a team of about 40 people, supplier relationships, delivery KPIs, a WMS implementation and logistics support for the company's expansion into Germany.

The operation needed three things: high order precision, replenishment from the external warehouse arriving on time, and data to base decisions on. I was the manager responsible for the floor – and I also built the tools.

What I built

1. Stock tracking that runs every day

An add-on for Google Sheets, written in Google Apps Script with Claude Code. It monitors inventory levels and triggers replenishment from the external warehouse in time. The team uses it every day in production.

2. One place for every operational event

I introduced OpenSearch as a central event store. It processes tens of thousands of events a day: orders, goods movements, sales, picker walking distance, packaging consumption and external data. This made operational statistics possible and gave AI-supported decisions something real to work with.

One example: the data showed a correlation between weather and footfall in the brick-and-mortar store. Moderately poor weather increased in-store purchasing, severe weather reduced it. We used this as an input for stock planning.

3. An agent for supplier sourcing

With Claude Code I automated supplier sourcing. The agent identified qualifying vendors and issued requests for quotation against my parameters – dates, technical specifications and additional services such as installation.

4. Teaching the team

Tools only help if people use them. I trained about ten colleagues in the effective use of Claude Cowork.

Improvements on the floor

Not everything was AI. The same approach – observe, measure, change – drove the physical work too:

  • Goods placement re-organised with an ABC analysis based on sales volume, cutting the walking distance pickers cover per order.
  • The in-house picking app improved step by step: distinct audio feedback for correct and incorrect scans, so pickers notice errors without looking at the screen, plus warehouse location support.
  • A 2-layer warehouse with an external overflow site; most box deliveries routed to the external warehouse and brought in together with goods, freeing significant space in the main warehouse.
  • Packing stations rebuilt by hand into purpose-built workstations.

Results

  • 99+% order fulfilment precision.
  • Stock-tracking add-on in daily production use.
  • Tens of thousands of operational events a day in one searchable place.
  • CZK 500,000 a year saved through negotiations with shipping providers.
  • About ten colleagues working with AI on their own.

What I would do differently

I would measure the impact of every automation from day one. I did not set baselines, so I can show that the tools are in daily use, but not exactly how many hours they save. On a client engagement, the baseline comes first – before anything gets automated.

What this means for your operation

  • Start on the floor, not in a slide deck: find where people lose time or make errors.
  • Build small tools that the team uses every day, then grow them.
  • Put operational events in one place, so decisions rest on data.
  • Train people, so the tools keep working after the project ends.
